Research Orientation

Language models as learners, not just speakers

Much of machine learning is organised around endpoints — benchmarks, leaderboards, final accuracy. My work is motivated by a discomfort with this framing. I approach language models instead as epistemic artefacts, focusing on learning dynamics rather than end states, and drawing on tools from psychometrics and developmental psychology.

Small language models are not merely scaled-down versions of larger ones. Their value lies less in what they achieve than in what they make intelligible.

Research

Key Themes

Data-efficient small language models and cognitively-inspired AI — combining developmental interpretability, multilinguality, and the linguistic and information-theoretic study of language.

Cognitively-Inspired Design & Evaluation

Developmentally plausible models, interpretability, and evaluation grounded in how humans acquire language.

Pretraining & Interpretability

Learning dynamics and developmental interpretability of small LMs — via the Pico framework.

Multilinguality

Human-scale multilingual modelling, cross-lingual representations, and bilingual learner competence.

Tokenization

Information-driven, language-adaptive subword tokenisation (e.g. ByteSpan).

Alignment & Interaction

Pedagogical alignment, contingent interaction, and cognitively-inspired student proxies.

Cognitive Science & Linguistics

Neo-emergentist syntax, grammar induction, and information-theoretic models of cross-lingual phonology.
