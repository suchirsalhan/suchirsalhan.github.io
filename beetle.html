Beetle: A new way to experimentally study how language models learn.

What if we could study not just what language models know, but how their language experience shapes what they learn?

I created Beetle, an open-source experimental framework that lets researchers systematically manipulate the conditions under which language models develop: which languages they encounter, how much data they see, which tokenizer they use, and in what order they learn different languages. The result is a collection of 330 open models, spanning 13 first languages, five exposure curricula and three training scales, with dense checkpoints that make representational change measurable throughout training. This provides researchers with a new testbed for investigating how linguistic representations emerge, converge and diverge under different learning experiences — turning questions about language acquisition and multilingual model development into controlled, reproducible experiments.
