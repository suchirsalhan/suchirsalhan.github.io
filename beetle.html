Beetle: A new way to experimentally study how language models learn.

What if we could study not just what language models know, but how their language experience shapes what they learn?

I created Beetle, an open-source experimental framework that lets researchers systematically manipulate the conditions under which language models develop: which languages they encounter, how much data they see, which tokenizer they use, and in what order they learn different languages. The result is a collection of 330 open models, spanning 13 first languages, five exposure curricula and three training scales, with dense checkpoints that make representational change measurable throughout training. This provides researchers with a new testbed for investigating how linguistic representations emerge, converge and diverge under different learning experiences — turning questions about language acquisition and multilingual model development into controlled, reproducible experiments.

  • Designed and built Beetle, an open-source framework for controlled bilingual and multilingual pretraining, with a declarative YAML interface for independently manipulating tokenisation, target language, training budgets and exposure curricula.
  • Designed, trained and released a 330-model suite (285 bilingual, 45 monolingual), spanning 13 first languages, five exposure curricula and three training scales (100M–24B tokens), with densely checkpointed training trajectories for mechanistic analysis.
  • Developed five controlled exposure curricula (B1–B5) to isolate how balanced, simultaneous, sequential, episodic and late second-language exposure shape acquisition, enabling systematic experiments on learning dynamics, phase transitions and catastrophic forgetting.
  • Released an end-to-end reproducibility and interpretability infrastructure, including three pip-installable libraries, training data, tokenisers, model checkpoints and analysis tooling, preserving gradients, activations and circuits for studying how representations and linguistic abilities emerge throughout training.
